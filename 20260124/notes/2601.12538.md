# Agentic Reasoning for Large Language Models

## 1. One-Line Contribution
A survey that frames agentic reasoning for LLMs, organizing methods into foundational, self-evolving, and collective layers, distinguishing in-context vs post-training optimization, and reviewing applications, benchmarks, and open problems.

---

## 2. Problem the Paper Solves
LLM reasoning excels in closed-world tasks but struggles in open-ended, dynamic environments; the paper addresses the lack of a systematic roadmap for agentic reasoning that unifies planning, tool use, feedback, memory, and multi-agent collaboration.

---

## 3. Proposed Solution / Architecture
The paper proposes a taxonomy of agentic reasoning along three scopes: foundational agentic reasoning (planning, tool use, and search in stable environments), self-evolving agentic reasoning (feedback, memory, and adaptation), and collective multi-agent reasoning (role assignment, communication, and shared goals). Across these layers it distinguishes in-context reasoning (structured orchestration and adaptive workflows at inference time) from post-training reasoning (reinforcement learning and fine-tuning to internalize behaviors). It also surveys applications and benchmarks, and outlines open challenges.

---

## 4. Key Concepts Introduced
- Agentic reasoning taxonomy (foundational, self-evolving, collective)
- In-context reasoning vs post-training reasoning
- Reasoning-driven orchestration across planning, tool use, search, memory, and collaboration

---

## 5. Relation to AI Agents / Agent Governance
It provides a structured roadmap for designing and evaluating LLM agents, including collaboration and feedback mechanisms, and highlights governance as an open challenge for real-world deployment.

## 6. Strengths

- Connects methods to applications and benchmarks while outlining future directions
